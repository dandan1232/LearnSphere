## Design Context

### Users

LearnSphere is designed first for technical self-learners: developers, university students, and AI learners who study from public technical documentation, open-source books, and structured online tutorials. Their core job is to turn passive reading into active recall through AI-generated quizzes, immediate feedback, saved attempts, and contextual tutoring tied to the exact source material and question.

### Brand Personality

The product should feel playful, youthful, and game-like without becoming childish or careless. It should create momentum, curiosity, and the satisfaction of visible progress. The AI tutor remains patient and trustworthy even when the surrounding quiz experience is energetic.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a high-energy learning-game direction inspired by the immediacy and feedback of Kahoot, adapted for focused individual study. Support both light and dark themes. Build the identity around cobalt blue, acid lime, and coral orange, balanced by warm tinted neutrals and strong text contrast. Prefer bold typography, purposeful geometric shapes, clear score feedback, and a few memorable motion moments.

Explicitly avoid generic AI-product aesthetics: no purple-to-blue gradients, neon-on-dark defaults, decorative glassmorphism, glowing chatbot orbs, generic equal-card grids, or oversized marketing copy. The interface should look deliberately art-directed rather than template-generated.

### Design Principles

1. Make learning feel active: every answer should produce immediate, legible feedback and forward momentum.
2. Keep the source trustworthy: explanations and AI tutoring must remain visually connected to the relevant question and source passage.
3. Use game energy selectively: reserve strong color and motion for progress, scoring, streaks, and important state changes.
4. Preserve focus: reading, answering, and reviewing should each have a clear primary action without nested-card clutter.
5. Design inclusively: meet WCAG AA contrast, never rely on color alone, support keyboard navigation and reduced motion, and keep critical functionality available on mobile.
