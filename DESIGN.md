# LearnSphere Design System

LearnSphere should feel like a well-used learning field guide: direct, energetic, and made for people who want to prove what they understand. It is not an AI showcase. The model works behind the desk; the learner, source material, questions, and evidence stay in front.

## Design idea

**Learning Lab / 学习实验室** combines three useful patterns from `awesome-design-md`:

- PostHog's friendly engineering-manual structure: warm paper, crisp rules, practical callouts.
- Figma's editorial color blocks: color creates hierarchy instead of decorative shadows.
- Miro's board-like organization: chapters and choices behave like material that can be sorted and selected.

These are references, not brand templates. Never copy their logos, mascots, exact palettes, or proprietary type choices.

## Visual language

- Canvas: warm paper neutrals, never pure white or pure black.
- Ink: blue-tinted near black with strong readable contrast.
- Primary: cobalt blue for forward actions and active states.
- Memory cue: acid lime for progress, confirmation, and successful recall.
- Correction cue: coral for question types, review, and important warnings.
- Typography: Bricolage Grotesque for Latin display text and numbers; Noto Sans SC for Chinese and reading text. Headlines are compact and editorial, not oversized marketing slogans.
- Shape: mostly square or lightly softened corners. Pills are reserved for compact status labels.
- Depth: borders, overlap, and flat color blocks first. Shadows are short and mechanical, never glowing or atmospheric.

## Interaction rules

1. Keep the primary learning action reachable without hunting or scrolling.
2. Treat chapters and answers as rows or working material, not a grid of generic cards.
3. Use motion only to explain progress, state changes, and navigation.
4. Keep AI language out of primary navigation and headlines unless the model itself is the object being configured.
5. Every explanation remains connected to its question and source locator.
6. Light and dark themes must preserve the same hierarchy; dark mode is not a neon variant.

## Page patterns

- Home: editorial introduction plus one bold challenge sheet; the link input is the obvious starting point.
- Import: a source worksheet with a numbered chapter ledger and a persistent confirmation action.
- Configure: numbered sections and compact practice rows; selection uses color and position rather than floating cards.
- Quiz: question content scrolls, while progress and previous/next controls remain reliably reachable.
- Results: score is evidence, not decoration; explanations and source excerpts receive more space than celebratory effects.

## Avoid

- Purple-blue gradients, glowing AI orbs, glass panels, and synthetic mesh backgrounds.
- Repeated icon-heading-description cards of identical size.
- Excessive pills, huge empty hero areas, and generic copy such as “unlock your potential.”
- Calling every model-driven action “AI-powered.” Describe the learner outcome instead.
- Motion that shifts controls away from the pointer or keyboard focus.
